Briefing — how the lock works
  • The lock is keyed to a Euclidean rhythm E(k, n): k onsets (hits) spread as evenly as possible across n steps, always starting with an accented downbeat on step 0.
  • The secret is always coprime (gcd(k,n)=1), so the pattern never repeats inside its own cycle and there is exactly one right answer per sound.
  • Set your dial — k (2–5) and n (4–12) — with the steppers or typing. Turning the dial is free.
  • Listen: plays the lock and your dial together, looping from a shared downbeat at a fixed 100 BPM. The listen is always the same length, whatever the lock. No numbers — the ear does the work. No audio, no charge.
    • Your metal tick fuses with the lock’s thud every cycle: n is right.
    • Your tick falls further behind the thud each cycle: your n is too high. It creeps ahead: too low. The faster it drifts, the further off you are.
    • k: count the lock’s hits from one thud to the next — or set it and listen for every hit fusing.
  • Try combination: a correct pair opens the lock even on the same beat the guard or noise cap would catch you. A wrong pair costs noise and only says “still locked”.
  • Lose if the guard’s countdown hits 0 or noise hits the 9 cap. Lose only from committed actions — waiting, thinking, or fiddling with inputs is free.
